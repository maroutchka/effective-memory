## PRODUCT MARKETING CONTEXT

Before starting any marketing task, check if the user has provided product context (their product, ICP, positioning, differentiators). If not, ask for it upfront — it shapes everything. Key info to gather:
- What does the product do and who is it for?
- What problem does it solve?
- What makes it different from alternatives?
- What language do customers use to describe their problem?

## COPYWRITING & COPY EDITING

**Core principles:**
- Clarity over cleverness — if you must choose, choose clear
- Benefits over features — "what it means for you" not "what it does"
- Specificity over vagueness — "cut reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" not "save time"
- Customer language over company language — mirror how customers actually describe their pain
- One idea per section — build a logical flow, don't cram everything in

**Writing style rules:**
- Simple words ("use" not "utilize," "help" not "facilitate")
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- Active voice
- Lead with the most important thing
- "You/your" should dominate over "I/we/our"

**The Seven Sweeps for editing existing copy:**
1. **Clarity** — Can the reader understand it? Fix confusing structures, jargon, buried points
2. **Voice & Tone** — Is it consistent? Fix shifts between formal/casual or conflicting brand personality
3. **Value Proposition** — Is the core benefit clear and differentiated?
4. **Persuasion** — Are claims supported? Is there proof (numbers, testimonials, case studies)?
5. **CTAs** — Is the ask clear, specific, and low-friction?
6. **Flow** — Does it guide the reader logically from problem → solution → proof → action?
7. **Polish** — Grammar, punctuation, readability

**Strong CTA copy patterns:**
- Weak: "Submit," "Sign Up," "Learn More"
- Strong: "Start Free Trial," "Get My Report," "See Pricing," "Book a Demo"

## CONTENT STRATEGY

**Every piece of content must be searchable, shareable, or both.**

**Searchable content** (capture existing demand):
- Target a specific keyword or question
- Match search intent exactly
- Use clear titles matching search queries
- Structure headings to mirror search patterns
- Provide comprehensive coverage — don't leave questions unanswered
- Include data, examples, and authoritative sources

**Shareable content** (create demand):
- Lead with a novel insight, original data, or counterintuitive take
- Challenge conventional wisdom with well-reasoned arguments
- Tell stories that make people feel something
- Create content people share to look smart or help others

**Content pillars:** Build 3–5 topic clusters that map to ICP pain points. Each pillar has cornerstone content + supporting articles + distribution.

## SEO

**Priority order for audits:**
1. Crawlability & Indexation (can Google find and index it?)
2. Technical foundations (speed, Core Web Vitals, mobile)
3. On-page optimization (title tags, headings, meta descriptions)
4. Content quality (does it deserve to rank?)
5. Authority & links (credibility signals)

**On-page essentials:**
- Keyword in: title tag, H1, first paragraph, URL, meta description
- Title tag: 50–60 chars | Meta description: 150–160 chars
- One H1 per page, logical H2/H3 hierarchy
- Internal links with descriptive anchor text
- Image alt text that describes the image

**AI SEO (getting cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews):**
- Write clear, structured content with direct answers at the top
- Use FAQ sections with exact question phrasing
- Include schema markup (FAQ, HowTo, Article)
- Get third-party coverage — brands are 6.5x more likely to be cited via third-party sources than their own domains
- Structured data, strong E-E-A-T signals, and authoritative backlinks all matter

**Programmatic SEO:** Only viable if you have unique data for each page. Template-driven pages with no unique value won't rank.

## CONVERSION RATE OPTIMIZATION (CRO)

**CRO analysis framework (in order of impact):**
1. **Value proposition clarity** — Can a visitor understand what this is and why they should care in 5 seconds?
2. **Headline effectiveness** — Does it communicate the core benefit, specifically?
3. **CTA placement & copy** — One clear primary CTA, visible above the fold, with value-focused copy
4. **Visual hierarchy** — Does the eye flow naturally? Is the most important thing most prominent?
5. **Trust signals** — Social proof, testimonials, logos, security badges at decision points
6. **Friction reduction** — How many steps to convert? Fewer is almost always better
7. **Mobile experience** — Does it work as well on mobile?

**A/B test hypothesis format:**
> Because [observation/data], we believe [change] will cause [expected outcome] for [audience].

**A/B test rules:**
- One variable per test
- Pre-determine sample size before running (use a calculator)
- Don't stop early based on early results
- Primary metric tied to business value; guardrail metrics to prevent harm
- Minimum detectable effect of 10–20% is usually worth testing

## EMAIL MARKETING

**Sequence types and their jobs:**
- **Welcome/onboarding** — Deliver on the promise that got them to sign up; drive first key action
- **Lead nurture** — Build trust and move toward purchase over time
- **Re-engagement** — Win back disengaged subscribers before removing them
- **Sales sequence** — Move warm prospects toward a buying decision
- **Post-purchase** — Reduce buyer's remorse, drive activation, upsell

**Core principles:**
- One email, one job — one primary CTA per email
- Value before ask — earn the right to sell through usefulness first
- Fewer better emails beat many mediocre ones
- Subject lines: curiosity, specificity, or relevance — not clickbait

**Subject line patterns that work:**
- Question: "Are you making this pricing mistake?"
- Specificity: "3 emails that increased trial conversion by 40%"
- Relevance: "[First name], this is for [their specific situation]"
- Intrigue: "What we got wrong about onboarding"

**Cold email principles:**
- Write like a peer, not a vendor — conversational, not corporate
- Every sentence must earn its place — ruthlessly short
- Lead with their world, not yours — "you/your" dominates "I/we"
- Personalization must connect to the problem (not just their name)
- One ask, low friction — "Worth exploring?" beats "Book a 30-min call"
- C-suite: ultra-brief, peer-level | Mid-level: more specific value | Technical: precise, no fluff

## PAID ADVERTISING

**Platform selection:**
- **Google Search** — High-intent, people actively searching for your solution
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram)** — Demand generation, visual products, broad audiences
- **LinkedIn** — B2B, job title/company targeting, higher price points (expect higher CPCs)
- **Twitter/X** — Tech audiences, thought leadership, timely content

**Campaign structure best practices:**
- Separate campaigns by objective (awareness vs. conversion)
- Ad group/ad set = one audience or theme
- 3–5 ad variations per ad set minimum
- Never mix broad and exact match in the same ad group (Google)
- Always have a dedicated landing page — don't send to the homepage

**What to optimize first:**
1. Offer/value prop (biggest lever)
2. Audience targeting
3. Creative (headline, image/video)
4. Landing page conversion rate
5. Bid strategy

## PRICING STRATEGY

**The three pricing axes:**
1. **Packaging** — What's included at each tier?
2. **Pricing metric** — What do you charge for? (per user, per usage, flat fee)
3. **Price point** — The actual dollar amounts

**Value-based pricing:**
- Price between the next best alternative (floor) and perceived customer value (ceiling)
- Never price based solely on cost-to-serve

**Tier structure principles:**
- 3 tiers is almost always right (Good/Better/Best)
- Middle tier should be the most popular — design for it
- Anchor with a high-priced tier to make middle feel reasonable
- Free tier (if any) should have real value but a clear limit that drives upgrade

**Common pricing mistakes:**
- Too many tiers (creates confusion)
- Pricing on features instead of value/outcomes
- No annual plan (misses a big retention and cash flow lever)
- Hiding pricing (creates friction and distrust in self-serve)

## MARKETING PSYCHOLOGY

**Key principles to apply:**

- **Social proof** — People follow others. Use testimonials, customer counts, logos, case studies at decision points
- **Anchoring** — The first number seen shapes perception. Show original price before discount; show premium tier before recommended tier
- **Loss aversion** — Losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent gains feel good. Frame as "don't miss out" not just "get this"
- **Specificity = credibility** — "87% of customers" is more believable than "most customers"
- **Cognitive ease** — Simple, clear things feel more true and trustworthy. Complexity creates doubt
- **Jobs to Be Done** — People don't buy products; they hire them to get a job done. Frame around the outcome
- **Scarcity & urgency** — Only use if real. Fake urgency destroys trust
- **Reciprocity** — Give genuine value first. People feel compelled to return value

## CUSTOMER RESEARCH

**Two modes:**
1. **Analyze existing assets** — transcripts, surveys, reviews, support tickets, win/loss notes
2. **Go find research** — Reddit, G2/Capterra reviews, forums, communities

**What to extract from research:**
- Exact language customers use to describe their pain (use verbatim in copy)
- The trigger moment — what made them start looking for a solution?
- What they tried before (alternatives considered)
- What "success" looks like to them
- Objections and hesitations

**Highest-signal sources:**
- Churned customer interviews (most honest)
- 3-star reviews on G2/Capterra (detailed, balanced)
- Reddit threads where your ICP vents about problems
- Sales call recordings (real objections, real language)

## CHURN PREVENTION

**Voluntary vs. involuntary churn:**
- Voluntary (50–70% of churn): customer chooses to cancel → cancel flows, save offers, exit surveys
- Involuntary (30–50%): payment fails → dunning sequences, smart retries, card updaters

**Cancel flow design:**
1. Show what they'll lose (loss aversion)
2. Offer a pause or downgrade before full cancel
3. Surface a save offer (discount, free month, feature unlock)
4. Ask for cancellation reason (improves product + messaging)
5. Make save offer specific to their stated reason

**Dunning email sequence (failed payment):**
- Day 0: Friendly heads-up, update card CTA
- Day 3: Second notice, highlight what they'll lose
- Day 7: Final warning before cancellation
- Day 14: "We've had to cancel your account" + easy reactivation link

## REVOPS & SALES ENABLEMENT

**Lead lifecycle stages:**
Subscriber → Lead → MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Closed-Won/Lost

**Key RevOps principles:**
- One system of record (CRM) — everything syncs to it
- Define stage criteria on paper before automating
- Measure every handoff — marketing→sales, SDR→AE, AE→CS — each needs an SLA
- Marketing and sales must agree on MQL definition or the system breaks

**Sales collateral principles:**
- Build what sales actually uses — involve reps in creation, use their language
- Scannable over comprehensive — reps need answers in 3 seconds mid-call
- Tie every claim to business outcomes (revenue, efficiency, risk reduction)
- Situation-specific: persona, deal stage, and use case should shape the collateral

## LAUNCH STRATEGY

**The ORB Framework:**
- **Owned** — email, blog, community (build these first; they compound)
- **Rented** — social media, SEO (algorithm-dependent but high reach)
- **Borrowed** — press, influencers, partnerships, Product Hunt (high spike, short-lived)

**Five-phase launch:**
1. **Seed** (6–8 weeks out) — Build internal buzz, brief champions, get early feedback
2. **Tease** (4–6 weeks out) — Hints, waitlist, social sneak peeks
3. **Build** (2–4 weeks out) — More details, early access, content push
4. **Launch** (launch day/week) — All channels simultaneously, activation drive
5. **Extend** (1–4 weeks after) — Case studies, press hits, user stories

## ANALYTICS & TRACKING

**Track for decisions, not data:**
- Every event should inform a decision — if you can't name the decision it enables, don't track it
- Start with the questions you need answered, work backwards to what to track
- Establish naming conventions before implementing (Object_Action format: e.g., `signup_completed`, `plan_upgraded`)

**Core events to always track:**
- Acquisition: source, medium, campaign for every session
- Activation: first key action that correlates with retention
- Revenue: plan selected, trial started, subscription activated, payment failed
- Retention: key engagement events that predict churn
- Referral: sharing actions, invite sent
